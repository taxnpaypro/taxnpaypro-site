Nanny Tax 2026: Do You Owe It, and How to Work Out the Schedule H and W-2 Numbers Yourself

↓ Get the free Excel + PDF kit for this article

If you pay a nanny, babysitter, caregiver for a parent, housekeeper or private gardener, you may be a household employer in the eyes of the IRS. That brings a short list of yearly duties: withholding social security and Medicare, paying federal unemployment tax, giving your worker a Form W-2 and filing Schedule H with your own return. Many families only find out in April, when the paperwork is late and the tax is due all at once.

This guide walks you through the 2026 thresholds, the exceptions, a step-by-step calculation you can do in a spreadsheet, and the dates for 2026 wages.

Who this is for: families who pay someone to work in or around their home, including caregivers for children or aging parents, and want to know if the "nanny tax" applies for 2026.

Step 1. Is the worker your employee?

A household worker is usually your employee if you control not only what work is done but how it is done. Two common situations where the worker is not your employee:

  • Agency workers. If an agency provides the worker and controls what work is done and how, the worker isn't your employee.
  • Self-employed providers. A worker who provides childcare in their own home is generally not your employee, and a contractor who controls how the job is done (a lawn service with its own crew and tools, for example) is self-employed.

"Cash wages" includes checks, bank transfers and payment apps, not just paper money. The two tests are separate: you can owe social security and Medicare without owing FUTA, and the reverse.

Wages that don't count

For social security and Medicare, do not count wages you pay to:

  • your spouse;
  • your child under age 21;
  • your parent, unless both of these apply: your parent cares for your child who is under 18 (or has a condition needing personal care for at least 4 continuous weeks in a quarter), and you are divorced and not remarried, widowed, or living with a spouse who cannot care for the child because of a physical or mental condition;
  • an employee who is under 18 at any time during the year, unless household work is their principal occupation. A student's household work is not considered their principal occupation.

For FUTA, only the spouse, child-under-21 and parent exclusions apply. A teenage babysitter can therefore be exempt from social security and Medicare but still count toward the $1,000 FUTA test.

Step 3. Set up before the first payday

  • Get an EIN. You must put an employer identification number on the forms you file for a household employee. Your SSN is not a substitute.
  • Decide on income tax withholding. You are not required to withhold federal income tax. You withhold it only if your employee asks and you agree (the employee gives you Form W-4).
  • Check your state. State unemployment registration, thresholds and paid-leave or disability programs vary. Look up your state's workforce agency.

Step 4. Track pay and withholding in a spreadsheet

One row per payday: A date, B hours, C cash wages, plus formula columns. In row 2:

D  Quarter:              =ROUNDUP(MONTH(A2)/3, 0)
E  Year-to-date wages:   =SUM($C$2:C2)
F  Soc. sec. withheld:   =IF($K$1>=3000, ROUND(C2*0.062, 2), 0)
G  Medicare withheld:    =IF($K$1>=3000, ROUND(C2*0.0145, 2), 0)
H  Net pay:              =C2 - F2 - G2 - [income tax if agreed]

Put your expected 2026 total cash wages in K1. If you are not sure you will reach $3,000, keep track and start withholding once it is clear, or plan with your worker in advance; Publication 926 explains how to handle amounts you did not withhold. For the FUTA test, a quarter summary does the job:

Quarter total:   =SUMIFS($C:$C, $D:$D, 1)      (repeat for quarters 2 to 4)
FUTA due?:       =IF(MAX(Q1:Q4)>=1000, "Yes", "No")

Step 5. Add it up for Schedule H and the W-2

At year-end, the totals map to Schedule H and the W-2:

Social security wages (W-2 box 3):  =MIN(SUM(C:C), 184500)
Medicare wages (W-2 box 5):         =SUM(C:C)
Soc. sec. + Medicare (both halves): =ROUND(SUM(C:C)*0.153, 2)   (below the wage base)
FUTA (net 0.6%):                    =ROUND(MIN(SUM(C:C), 7000)*0.006, 2)

The 0.6% net FUTA rate assumes you get the full 5.4% credit for paying state unemployment contributions on time (by April 15, 2027 for 2026); it can be lower in a credit reduction state.

Pay as you go, not all in April

Schedule H tax is paid with your Form 1040, so it can create an underpayment penalty if you wait. Publication 926 suggests two ways to cover it during the year: give your own employer a new Form W-4 asking for more withholding from your paycheck, or make estimated tax payments (for 2026: April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027).

Common mistakes

  • Treating a regular nanny as a contractor. If you set the schedule and decide how the work is done, the worker is usually your employee.
  • Counting only paper cash. Checks, Zelle and other transfers are cash wages.
  • Forgetting FUTA for a teenage babysitter. The under-18 exception covers social security and Medicare, not FUTA.
  • Using your SSN instead of an EIN on the W-2 and Schedule H.
  • Waiting until April to pay. Raise your own withholding or make estimated payments.
  • Ignoring the state. State unemployment and other programs have their own rules and deadlines.

FAQ

We paid a babysitter $2,800 for the whole year. Do we owe anything?

For social security and Medicare, no: $2,800 is below the 2026 $3,000 threshold. Check FUTA too: if any single quarter reached $1,000 (say $1,200 in the summer quarter), FUTA applies even though the yearly total is under $3,000.

My mother watches our kids and we pay her. Is that household employment?

Wages to your parent are excluded from social security and Medicare unless both special conditions apply (child under 18 or needing care, and you are divorced and not remarried, widowed, or your spouse can't provide the care). Wages to a parent are never FUTA wages.

Do I have to withhold income tax?

No. Federal income tax withholding for a household employee is voluntary and only happens if the employee asks and you agree.

What if the nanny leaves in October?

You still give a W-2 for the year. If the employee asks for it early, give it within 30 days after the request or the last wage payment, whichever is later.

Can I pay the employee's share instead of withholding it?

You can, but it changes how wages are reported. Read the section on this in Publication 926 before doing it.

Quick checklist

  • ☐ Exceptions checked (spouse, child under 21, parent, under-18)
  • ☐ EIN obtained; state requirements checked
  • ☐ 7.65% withheld each payday (if the $3,000 test is met)
  • ☐ W-2 and W-3 by February 1, 2027
  • ☐ Schedule H with your 2026 return by April 15, 2027
  • ☐ Extra W-4 withholding or estimated payments to cover it
